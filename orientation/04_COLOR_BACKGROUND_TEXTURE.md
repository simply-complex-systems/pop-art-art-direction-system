## LOAD / INSPECT

Load 04_COLOR_BACKGROUND_TEXTURE.pdf only. Inspect the relevant approved references below, with their governance metadata. Do not load unrelated sheets.

## PURPOSE

Teach contrast-first structured color and the three background axes.

## WHAT THIS STAGE GOVERNS

Color relationships, role importance, figure-ground distinction, field, surface and print finish.

## WHAT THIS STAGE DOES NOT GOVERN

Mandatory triads, mandatory light backgrounds, identity or Theme.

## BOUNDED CONCEPTS

SYSTEM RULE: Core color role IDs are generic. Optional display labels may describe a material or semantic use without turning it into a governing rule. Distinct MAJOR roles should normally maintain meaningful perceptual distinction even without adjacency metadata. Touching, overlap, a shared focal region and likely merging increase separation priority. MINOR accents may repeat more freely.

Evaluate every background role against the complete set of major subject roles. Compare hue, value and chroma together, using perceptual relationships rather than names alone. Intentional separators such as a contour or intervening gap can support readability. A high-value background is useful guidance, not a requirement.

Custom artist choices are unrestricted. Similar major-role colors can produce an advisory; preserve the artist’s choice. The four historical triads are examples and Official presets only, never governing harmony rules.

Category 5 has three independent axes: background field construction, surface texture, and print finish/reproduction effects. Ben-Day dots are regular equal-size dots; halftone varies size/density to express tone; stipple is irregular. Misregistration is a controlled print-layer offset, not blur. State whether treatment applies to background, subject or globally.

## VISUAL EXERCISE

Create a neutral three-major-form study with a background. Compare distinct hue/value/chroma assignments against an intentionally near-matching pair. Add a separator in a third study. Hold geometry stable. Separately demonstrate field, surface and print finish so the artist can critique each axis.

Restate the bounded concepts before making the image(s). Preserve supplied approved exemplar bytes; the exercise is a new study, not a replacement for an approved original.
